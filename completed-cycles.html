From observation to meaningful learning.

Two retrospective planning cycle case studies reconstructed from my June 2025 placement observations, weekly curriculum plan and recollection of my teaching. These are not original submitted assessment records. Reflections and future extensions are retrospective.

Case study 01 · 2 June 2025

Clay modelling and sculpting

Observation

Two children requested additional clay. One rolled and shaped clay to represent a butterfly; the other flattened clay to make a pretend pizza and added pumpkin seeds as toppings, then shared the creation with the educator.

Analysis of learning

The children demonstrated purposeful manipulation, symbolic thinking, imagination, agency and oral communication.

Planning

Week 2 plan: support fine motor skills, creativity and oral language through clay and natural materials. Introduce shape and texture vocabulary, modelling techniques and opportunities to describe creations.

Implementation (confirmed recollection)

I facilitated the experience, provided materials and guidance, and encouraged children to describe their creations. Children chose their own designs and requested additional clay as needed.

Evaluation of observed learning

The observed rolling, flattening and shaping provide evidence of fine motor engagement. The children expressed their ideas verbally and used materials symbolically.

Retrospective reflection and future planning

I would extend the experience through a pretend pizza shop with menus, counting toppings and collaborative role-play. Open-ended materials allow children to retain ownership of their designs.

Framework alignment

EYLF 1, 3, 4 and 5; VEYLDF integrated teaching and learning approaches and respectful relationships; potential APST links 1.2, 2.1, 3.3, 5.1.

Case study 02 · 12 June 2025

Science exploration: colour mixing

Observation

A child mixing blue and yellow paint asked whether adding more blue would make a darker green. The educator invited the child to investigate through experimentation.

Analysis of learning

The question demonstrated curiosity, prediction, descriptive language and emerging cause-and-effect reasoning.

Planning

Week 2 plan (2–6 June): explore primary and secondary colours and shades through guided experiments, predictions and descriptive language. The related observation was recorded on 12 June.

Implementation (confirmed recollection)

I guided the children through the colour mixing experience without supplying the answer. The children independently discovered that adding more blue changed the shade of green.

Evaluation of observed learning

The children tested an idea and discovered a change in colour. This provides evidence of inquiry, independent discovery and emerging understanding of cause and effect.

Retrospective reflection and future planning

I would extend the experience using colour charts, comparing samples and inviting children to record predictions and discoveries. This supports a balance of intentional teaching and child-led inquiry.

Framework alignment

EYLF 4 and 5; VEYLDF integrated teaching and learning approaches and equity and diversity; potential APST links 1.2, 2.1, 3.3, 5.1, 3.6.

Evidence basis: Week 2 curriculum plan and placement observations dated 2 and 12 June 2025, supplemented by retrospective confirmation. Children’s names omitted.
