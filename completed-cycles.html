Documentation of planning cycle 01

Clay modelling and sculpting

Date of observation
2 June 2025
Number of children
Two children
Age group
Preschool (3–5 years)
Learning area
Creative arts
Student teacher
Viola Meli

Part 1 · Observe and analyse

Collect information – Observation

I observed two children working with clay. Child A requested more clay to make a butterfly and began rolling and shaping it. Child B requested more clay to make a pizza, flattened it and added pumpkin seeds to represent meat toppings. The child proudly invited me to taste the pretend pizza.

What do you understand about what you observed?

The children used open-ended materials to express their interests and represent familiar objects. Their choices and requests demonstrated agency and purposeful engagement.

What did you find interesting or extraordinary?

The use of pumpkin seeds as pretend toppings made symbolic thinking visible. Both children developed their own designs rather than following a predetermined model.

What learning is being demonstrated?

Fine motor coordination through rolling and flattening; cognitive development through sequencing and symbolic thinking; oral language through requests and descriptions; creativity and confidence through independent choices.

What are the interests and strengths of the children observed?

Child A showed interest in nature-inspired modelling and purposeful design. Child B showed interest in imaginative food play and communication. Both engaged in hands-on creative exploration.

What can you extend on?

A pretend pizza shop with menus and counting toppings; butterfly collages using natural materials; storytelling about clay creations.

Links to EYLF/VEYLDF learning outcomes

Outcome 1: identity and agency. Outcome 3: physical wellbeing and fine motor engagement. Outcome 4: creativity and purposeful exploration. Outcome 5: verbal and symbolic communication.

Part 2 · Plan, act and review

Plan – learning intentions

Week 2 curriculum plan: promote fine motor skills, creativity and oral language through clay and natural materials.

Resources and intentional teaching strategies

Clay, modelling tools, rolling pins and suitable natural materials. Introduce shape and texture vocabulary, model techniques as needed, encourage descriptions and maintain active supervision. These are planned strategies, not a record of every action taken.

Open-ended questions – suggested prompts

What would you like to make? How could you create that shape? Can you tell me about your creation?

Act and do – Implementation

I facilitated the experience, provided materials and guidance, and encouraged children to describe their creations. The children chose their designs, requested more clay and used rolling, shaping and flattening techniques.

Review and evaluate

The observed manipulation of clay provides evidence of fine motor engagement. The children communicated their intentions and used materials symbolically, supporting the learning intentions for creativity and oral language.

Critical reflection and future planning

This experience highlights the value of open-ended resources and child agency. I would extend the children’s interests through a pretend pizza restaurant incorporating literacy, counting and collaborative play, and offer further opportunities to describe their creations.

Professional framework alignment

VEYLDF: integrated teaching and learning approaches; respectful relationships and responsive engagement; assessment for learning and development. NQS Quality Area 1. Potential APST links: 1.2, 2.1, 3.3, 3.4, 5.1.

Documentation of planning cycle 02

Science exploration – colour mixing

Date of observation
12 June 2025
Number of children
One focus child within a group
Age group
Preschool (3–5 years)
Learning area
Science and creative arts
Student teacher
Viola Meli

Part 1 · Observe and analyse

Collect information – Observation

While mixing blue and yellow paint to make green, a child asked what would happen if more blue were added and predicted a darker green. I invited the children to investigate through experimentation. They independently discovered that adding more blue changed the shade of green (confirmed retrospective recollection).

What do you understand about what you observed?

The child demonstrated curiosity about the relationship between colour proportions and the resulting shade. The question provided an opportunity for responsive, inquiry-based teaching.

What did you find interesting or extraordinary?

The child formed a prediction rather than simply mixing colours. The children discovered the change themselves with educator guidance.

What learning is being demonstrated?

Scientific inquiry through prediction and experimentation; emerging cause-and-effect reasoning; descriptive language and questioning; creative exploration of colour; independent discovery.

What are the interests and strengths of the children observed?

The focus child showed curiosity, confidence in asking questions and interest in hands-on colour exploration.

What can you extend on?

Compare different paint proportions, introduce colour charts and picture books about colour, and invite children to record discoveries through drawings and samples.

Links to EYLF/VEYLDF learning outcomes

Outcome 4: curiosity, experimentation and problem-solving. Outcome 5: communicating questions, predictions and ideas.

Part 2 · Plan, act and review

Plan – learning intentions

Week 2 curriculum plan (2–6 June) includes colour mixing to explore primary and secondary colours and shades through guided experimentation. The related observation was recorded on 12 June. Learning intentions include prediction, investigation and descriptive language.

Resources and intentional teaching strategies

Non-toxic primary-colour paints, mixing trays, brushes, paper and aprons. Encourage predictions, introduce vocabulary such as lighter and darker, and scaffold investigation without supplying answers. These are planned strategies, not a record of every action taken.

Open-ended questions – suggested prompts

What might happen if we add more blue? How could we create a different shade? What differences can you see?

Act and do – Implementation

I guided the children through the colour mixing experience and encouraged them to investigate rather than giving them the answer. The children independently discovered that adding more blue changed the shade of green.

Review and evaluate

The children tested an idea and observed a change in colour. This supports the learning intentions for scientific inquiry and emerging cause-and-effect reasoning. The focus child’s questions provide evidence of communication and prediction.

Critical reflection and future planning

This experience highlights the balance between intentional teaching and child-led learning. I would extend the investigation using colour charts, comparisons of paint proportions and opportunities to document predictions and discoveries.

Professional framework alignment

VEYLDF: integrated teaching and learning approaches; equity and diversity; assessment for learning and development. NQS Quality Area 1. Potential APST links: 1.2, 2.1, 3.3, 3.4, 5.1, 3.6.

Evidence basis: Week 2 curriculum plan and placement observations dated 2 and 12 June 2025, supplemented by retrospective confirmation. Children’s names omitted.
